# Research notes: "The Lighthouse Hour" podcast

The Lighthouse Hour is a fictional weekly show about quiet, overlooked infrastructure.

## Topic file: Fog signals of the Northern Reach
- The Northern Reach coast had 14 foghorn stations by 1931; the last was switched off in 1987.
- The Brannock Point horn was audible for 11 miles and sounded two blasts every 45 seconds.
- Keepers logged 212 fog hours at Brannock Point in 1954, the worst year on record.
- Fishing crews said the horn pitch (a low B-flat) carried better than higher tones through dense fog.
- Replacement radar beacons cut shipwrecks on the Reach from 9 per decade to 1 per decade after 1975.

## People
- Odalys Verhoeven: retired keeper at Brannock Point (1961-1984), kept handwritten logbooks, willing to be interviewed.
- Dr. Tamsin Okoro: acoustics researcher at Halvard University, studied low-frequency propagation in fog.
- Bastien Quill: fisherman's-union historian, holds the 1950s crew letters.

## Listener data
- Average episode length is 34 minutes; listener drop-off peaks at minute 22.
- Episodes with a first-person guest story in the first 5 minutes retain 18 percent more listeners.
- Listener survey: 61 percent want more "sound-rich" episodes with archival audio.

## Known gaps
- No recording of the Brannock horn survives; only a 1969 radio mention.
- The 1987 shutdown reason is disputed: budget cuts vs. a noise complaint from a new housing estate.
